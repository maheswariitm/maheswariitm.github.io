01About me

A scientist working across scales

Better forecasts begin with a better picture of the coupled system.

I work in the Monsoon Mission Group at IITM, Pune, studying the processes that shape the Indian summer monsoon and its predictability. My research combines coupled modelling, torbulent flux parameterization, coupled data assimilation, and monsoon forecasting.

My work has included observing system experiments, seasonal prediction of monsoon onset, model intercomparison, and improving air–sea fluxes. I have also contributed to the development of India’s Monsoon Mission Coupled Forecast System version 2 (MMCFSv2) and to operational seasonal forecasting efforts.

03Featured research

From the ocean’s daily rhythm to seasonal climate

Putting the diurnal cycle into the forecast.

In coupled models, the timing and size of daily sea-surface temperature changes can shape atmospheric variability. Research with CFSv2 explored how representing the ocean’s diurnal cycle and improving air–sea fluxes affect simulations from the daily scale to the seasonal mean.

This work is part of a broader effort to understand model biases and improve the representation of physical processes relevant to monsoon prediction.
